# DI-01: Data Integration — A Theoretical Perspective

- **Primary reference:** Lenzerini, M. (2002). "Data Integration: A Theoretical
  Perspective." *Proceedings of the 21st ACM SIGMOD-SIGACT-SIGART Symposium on
  Principles of Database Systems (PODS 2002)*, 233–246. DOI: 10.1145/543613.543644.
- **Bibliographic details verified via:** DBLP (dblp.org/rec/conf/pods/Lenzerini02.html),
  2026-08-30.
- **Canonical topic:** Formal foundations of data integration
- **Used in:** Chapter 7 (integration policies)

## Key Points

- A **data integration system** I = (G, S, M) has three components: a **global schema**
  G, a set of **source schemas** S, and a **mapping** M between them.
- **GAV (global-as-view):** the global schema is expressed in terms of the sources.
- **LAV (local-as-view):** the sources are expressed in terms of the global schema.
- Mappings can be **sound** (source data may be a subset of what the mapping asserts),
  **complete** (a superset), or **exact** (both).
- Queries are posed over the global schema; the system answers them using data that
  resides in the sources.

## Semantic Contract

- Data integration is about reconciling heterogeneous sources under one global view —
  the formal motivation for the acquisition/integration pipeline in Ch7.
- Use it to teach: integration is a *mapping decision* with a *semantics* (sound /
  complete / exact), not a mechanical merge.
- Do NOT present integration as a solved, automatic problem; ambiguity and mapping
  choice are inherent.
