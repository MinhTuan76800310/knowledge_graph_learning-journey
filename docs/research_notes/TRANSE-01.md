# TRANSE-01: TransE — Translating Embeddings for Modeling Multi-relational Data

- **Primary reference:** Bordes, A., Usunier, N., Garcia-Duran, A., Weston, J., Yakhnenko, O. (2013). "Translating Embeddings for Modeling Multi-relational Data." *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 26 (NIPS 2013)*.
- **URL:** https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2013/hash/1cecc7a77928ca8133fa24680a88d2f9-Abstract.html
- **Status:** FETCHED_AND_VERIFIED (bibliographic metadata + abstract verified via the NeurIPS proceedings page, 2026-08-30; technical details follow the paper's published text)
- **Used in:** Chapter 8

## Key Points

- Goal: embed entities and relations of multi-relational data into low-dimensional vector spaces, modeling relationships "as translations operating on the low-dimensional embeddings of the entities".
- Core intuition: for a triple (h, r, t), the relation vector r acts as a translation, so that h + r ≈ t.
- Scoring function: f_r(h, t) = ||h + r − t|| (L1 or L2 norm); lower distance = more plausible triple.
- Training: positive triples from the knowledge base; negative/corrupted triples generated by randomly replacing the head or tail with another entity. Optimization via a margin-based ranking loss: the score of a positive triple is pushed below the score of corrupted candidates by a margin.
- Evaluation protocol: link prediction over held-out triples; metrics are mean rank and hits@10, reported in both *raw* and *filtered* settings (in filtered evaluation, other known positive triples are removed from the ranked candidates before scoring the target).
- Scales to large data sets (1M entities, 25k relations, 17M+ training samples in the paper).
- Well-documented limitation: translational geometry conflicts with 1-to-N, N-to-1, N-to-N and symmetric relation patterns, where the same relation vector cannot map one head to many distinct tails simultaneously.

## Semantic Contract

- TransE's score is a geometric heuristic; it is not a probability of truth.
- Corrupted triples are *training devices* (assumed negatives), not verified false statements.
- TransE is pedagogically useful but not current SOTA; the book presents it as a design family example (translational geometry).
- MUST NOT: present TransE as universally superior; say h + r ≈ t is a semantic equality.
