- **Primary reference:** Robertson, S. & Zaragoza, H. (2009). The Probabilistic Relevance Framework: BM25 and Beyond. Foundations and Trends in Information Retrieval 3(4), 333-389.
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1561/1500000019
- **Status:** FETCHED_AND_VERIFIED (bibliographic record + content read, 2026-08-30)
- **Used in:** Chapter 9
- **Canonical topic:** BM25 — lexical ranking derived from the probabilistic relevance framework

## Key Points

- BM25 (Okapi BM25) scores a document for a query as a sum over matched terms of: idf x (term-frequency saturation) x (document-length normalization).
- Components: inverse document frequency (idf) downweights common terms; k1 controls term-frequency saturation; b controls document-length normalization (0 = none, 1 = full).
- Derived from the probabilistic relevance framework's term-independence and two-Poisson assumptions — engineering simplifications, not a truth probability.
- BM25 needs no training data and handles exact terminology well; it fails on synonyms/paraphrases with no lexical overlap.

## Semantic Contract

- BM25 scores are ranking utilities for lexical match, not probabilities that a document answers the question.
- MUST NOT: interpret BM25 score magnitude as confidence; claim BM25 captures semantics; use idf without explaining it.
